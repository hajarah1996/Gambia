HISTORY

The Gambia is the smallest country on mainland Africa, located along the Gambia River and almost surrounded by Senegal except for its Atlantic coast. The river has been central to its history, serving as a trade route for centuries. Before European contact, the area was part of powerful West African empires such as Ghana, Mali, and Songhai.

In the 15th century, Portuguese traders arrived, followed by the British and French, who competed for control. By the 18th century, Britain dominated the river trade, especially in enslaved people. The Gambia became a British colony in 1888 and was a key site in the transatlantic slave trade.

The country gained independence from Britain on 18 February 1965 with Dawda Jawara as its first Prime Minister and later President. In 1970, it became a republic. After Jawara’s long rule, Yahya Jammeh seized power in a 1994 coup and ruled until 2017, when Adama Barrow won elections and began democratic reforms.

Presidential History

Dawda Jawara (1970–1994)

Dawda Jawara became The Gambia’s first President when the country became a republic in 1970. His government is remembered for maintaining political stability, promoting education, and supporting agriculture. His long tenure ended in 1994 after a military coup.

Yahya Jammeh (1994–2017)

Yahya Jammeh ruled The Gambia for 22 years after seizing power in 1994. His presidency was marked by development projects but also human rights abuses and press restrictions.

Adama Barrow (2017–Present)

Adama Barrow was elected President in 2016, marking the first peaceful transfer of power in over 20 years. His administration focuses on democracy, reforms, and development.

GOVERNMENT OF THE GAMBIA
The Gambia is a democratic republic with a presidential system of government. The President serves as both the Head of State and Head of Government, elected by the people for a five-year term. The government is divided into three main branches: Executive: Led by the President and the Cabinet, responsible for implementing laws and running the country. Legislative: The National Assembly, made up of elected representatives, creates and passes laws. Judiciary: Independent courts ensure justice and interpret the constitution. Since regaining democracy in 2017, The Gambia has been working toward stronger institutions, transparency, and good governance.

CULTURE OF THE GAMBIA
The Gambia is a vibrant mix of traditions, music, and languages. People speak English officially, but local languages like Wolof, Mandinka, and Fula are widely used. Festivals, weddings, and holidays are celebrated with colorful clothing, lively music, and traditional dance.
It reflects centuries of tradition passed through generations and celebrates the harmony of diverse ethnic groups such as the Mandinka, Wolof, Fula, Jola, and Serahule.
Music and Dance
Music and dance are at the heart of Gambian life. Traditional rhythms played on instruments like the kora (a 21-string harp), balafon (wooden xylophone), and djembe drums accompany storytelling, ceremonies, and celebrations. Each ethnic group has its unique dance styles—energetic movements, rhythmic drumming, and expressive gestures that bring people together at weddings, naming ceremonies, and festivals.
Festivals and Traditions
Gambians celebrate a variety of cultural and religious festivals that strengthen family and community bonds. Important occasions include Tobaski (Eid al-Adha), Koriteh (Eid al-Fitr), and Independence Day. During these times, families share food, exchange gifts, and dress in vibrant traditional attire. Local festivals like Roots Homecoming and Banjul Demba Cultural Festival showcase the country’s heritage through dance, music, and cultural exhibitions.
Arts and Crafts
The Gambia’s artistic expression is visible in its vibrant crafts. Skilled artisans create hand-woven textiles, colorful batik fabrics, intricate wood carvings, and traditional jewelry. Basket weaving and pottery are common crafts that blend function and beauty, often inspired by nature and daily life. These artworks are not only souvenirs but also a reflection of the country’s creativity and identity.

Famous Gambian Dishes

The Gambia is known for its rich and flavorful cuisine, with dishes that reflect the country’s diverse cultures and love for communal eating. The most famous of all is Benechin, meaning “one pot” in Wolof, widely celebrated as The Gambia’s national dish. It’s a delicious blend of rice, vegetables, and fish or meat cooked together, creating deep, aromatic flavors. In recent years, Benechin has gained international recognition, even winning culinary competitions against Nigeria’s jollof rice and Ghana’s version — proudly earning The Gambia the title of having the best jollof in West Africa. Other favorites like Yassa, made with marinated chicken or fish in a tangy onion sauce, and Domoda, a rich peanut stew, showcase the creativity and warmth found in Gambian cooking.

Benechin

Benechin, meaning “one pot” in Wolof, is The Gambia’s national dish and an early form of jollof rice. It’s made with rice, vegetables, and fish or meat, all cooked together in one pot.

Yassa

Yassa is a flavorful onion and mustard-based stew made with marinated chicken or fish. It symbolizes hospitality and is often served to guests and at celebrations.

Domoda

Domoda is a peanut stew made with groundnut paste, vegetables, and meat or fish. It’s one of The Gambia’s most beloved national dishes and reflects its agricultural heritage.



GEOGRAPHY OF THE GAMBIA

The Gambia is the smallest country on mainland Africa, stretching along the banks of the Gambia River. It is surrounded by Senegal, except for its coastline on the Atlantic Ocean. The country enjoys a tropical climate with distinct dry and rainy seasons, making it rich in vegetation and wildlife.



ECONOMY OF THE GAMBIA

The Gambian economy relies heavily on agriculture, tourism, and trade. Groundnuts (peanuts) are the main cash crop, while fishing and livestock also play major roles. Tourism, known for the “Smiling Coast” brand, provides jobs and revenue, attracting visitors to the beaches and cultural heritage sites.



TOURISM IN THE GAMBIA
Kotu & Kololi Beaches

Kunta Kinteh Island

Abuko Nature Reserve

Home to monkeys, crocodiles, and birds, showcasing The Gambia’s rich biodiversity.



EDUCATION IN THE GAMBIA

Education in The Gambia includes basic, secondary, and tertiary levels, with a growing focus on ICT and vocational skills. The University of The Gambia and various technical schools help shape the country’s future leaders. Literacy rates are improving as education becomes more accessible across the country.
